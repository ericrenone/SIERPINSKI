### I.2 The Photonic Band Structure as the Bloch Decomposition

For a 1D photonic crystal with period $a$, the electromagnetic Bloch modes satisfy:

$$\mathbf{E}_{n,k}(\mathbf{r}) = e^{ikz} \mathbf{u}_{n,k}(\mathbf{r})$$

## Part IV · Leaky Modes: The ker(F) Radiation Loss

### IV.1 A Thought Experiment: The Waveguide That Bleeds Light

A standard optical fiber guides light by total internal reflection: the core has a higher refractive index than the cladding, and rays hitting the core-cladding interface at angles above the critical angle are perfectly reflected. No light escapes — the mode is fully guided.

But reduce the cladding thickness, or reduce the core-cladding index contrast, or operate at a frequency near cutoff — and the guided mode begins to **leak**. Light radiates outward through the cladding into the ambient. The mode is no longer perfectly guided; it is a **leaky mode**, with a complex propagation constant:

$$\beta = \beta_R + i\beta_I$$

where $\beta_R$ is the real phase constant (governing propagation speed) and $\beta_I > 0$ is the attenuation constant (governing radiation loss rate). The power in the guided mode decays as $e^{-2\beta_I z}$ along the propagation direction.

### IV.2 Leaky Modes as the col(F)/ker(F) Radiation Boundary

The TH(a,d) identification:

- **col(F)**: the guided component of the leaky mode — the part of the field that propagates along the waveguide with phase velocity $\omega/\beta_R$. This is the observable, coherent, transmitted signal.
- **ker(F)**: the radiated component — the part of the field that radiates transversely into the ambient medium. This is the hidden, incoherent, lost signal — Fisher information leaving the waveguide.
- **The radiation boundary**: the waveguide-cladding interface, where the guided and radiated components couple. The coupling strength determines $\beta_I$ — the Fisher-information loss rate through the boundary.

The leaky mode IS the TEMPUS equation applied to guided-wave optics: $dD/dt + J = 0$ where $D$ is the guided-mode power (the distinguishability of the guided signal from the ambient noise) and $J$ is the Fisher-information radiation rate $2\beta_I P_{\text{guided}}$. Time IS the propagation coordinate $z$; the Fisher information dissipates along the waveguide as the leaky mode radiates.

### IV.3 Photonic-Crystal Fibers: Bandgap Guidance vs. Leakage

In a photonic-crystal fiber (PCF), the cladding is a 2D photonic crystal. Two guidance mechanisms exist:

**Index-guiding PCF** (solid core, lower effective cladding index): guidance by modified total internal reflection. The effective index of the photonic-crystal cladding is lower than the core index, and guidance occurs as in a conventional fiber. Leaky modes exist at frequencies below the effective cutoff.

**Bandgap-guiding PCF** (hollow core): guidance by the photonic bandgap effect. Light at frequencies within the cladding bandgap cannot propagate in the cladding and is confined to the air core — even though the air core has a lower refractive index than the cladding material. This IS the photonic bandgap col(F)/ker(F) in its most dramatic form: light is guided in a region of *lower* refractive index because the surrounding photonic crystal forbids propagation at that frequency.

The hollow-core PCF IS the electromagnetic realization of the quantum well (DELIGNE, particle-in-a-box): the air core IS the well, the photonic crystal cladding IS the barrier, and the guided mode IS the bound state. The bandgap IS the barrier height, and the leaky modes at the bandgap edges correspond to quasi-bound states that tunnel through the finite-thickness photonic-crystal barrier — exactly the TUNNEL framework's evanescent-wave tunneling.

Recent work (2024–2026) on **anti-resonant hollow-core fibers** (ARHCFs) has achieved record-low propagation losses ($< 0.1$ dB/km at telecom wavelengths) by engineering the leaky-mode radiation loss to be exponentially small. The ARHCF IS the col(F)/ker(F) boundary optimized for minimum Fisher-information leakage: the anti-resonant cladding structure reflects light with near-perfect efficiency, minimizing the $\beta_I$ radiation loss while maintaining broadband guidance.
